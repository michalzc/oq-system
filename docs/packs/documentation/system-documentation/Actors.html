Player Character

The header holds the portrait (click it to change the image) and the character's name, gender, age, culture, rank, organisation and concept.

Characteristics

The panel shows the final value of each characteristic: base plus mod. The gear icon opens the Characteristics dialogue:

  • Each row has the base value, a roll formula with a dice icon, and a mod. The Roll All icon rolls every characteristic. Rolls are posted to chat and fill in the base values.
  • The points section follows the rules: Spent is the sum of base values minus 56, Remain is the points available minus the spent ones. Points available default to 30.
  • Nothing is saved until you click Save Data. Reset Data restores the current values.

Mod is a simple way to track temporary changes: it is added to the base value.

Attributes

Attributes are calculated from characteristics and items:

  • Hit Points: (SIZ + CON) / 2, rounded, plus mod.
  • Magic Points: POW plus mod.
  • Damage Modifier: from STR + SIZ. Up to 10: -1d6, up to 15: -1d4, up to 25: none, up to 30: +1d4, then +1d6 for 31-45, +2d6 for 46-60, and so on. The mod is text, for example +1d4 or 2, and is appended to the result.
  • Movement Rate: 15 plus mod. Effects change this; see Effects below.
  • Armour Worn: the highest Armour Points among armour in the Worn or Natural state, plus mod. Armour does not stack. Effects add to this; see Effects below.

The current Hit Points and Magic Points can be typed in, or adjusted by clicking the HP or MP label: left-click adds one, right-click removes one. A typed value above the maximum is stored as the maximum. Characters also track Fortune Points and Growth Points. The gear icon opens the Attributes dialogue, where only the mods can be changed.

Temporary Hit Points and Magic Points

Some spells and rulings give a creature Hit Points or Magic Points beyond its maximum, such as Vigour, 5 extra HP until sunrise or the Magic Points that Tap draws above the maximum. These are temporary points, kept apart from the ordinary points: the sheet shows the ordinary value against the maximum, then the temporary total in green, for example 6 / 10 +7. Hover over the green number to see it named.

Damage takes temporary points first: the oldest pool is emptied before the next, and only then are ordinary Hit Points lost. Right-clicking the HP label counts as one point of damage, so it removes a temporary point first. Healing, including left-clicking the HP label, restores ordinary Hit Points only and never refills a pool. Typing a new current value changes only the ordinary Hit Points. See the Chat page for applying damage and healing.

Spending Magic Points works the same way. Right-clicking the MP label, the Decrease Magic Points chat button and -N in the token HUD take temporary Magic Points first, oldest pool first, then ordinary Magic Points. Left-clicking the MP label, Increase Magic Points and +N restore ordinary Magic Points only, up to the maximum, and never create or refill a pool. Casting a spell still spends ordinary Magic Points only.

Each grant is its own pool, listed in the order it was granted. Hit Points and Magic Points have separate pools. The layers icon next to the Hit Points or Magic Points opens the Temporary Hit Points or Temporary Magic Points dialogue, available to anyone who can edit the sheet:

  • Grant: type an amount and an optional label, such as Vigour from Aldric, and click Grant or press Enter. The new pool goes after every existing one. Without a label it is called Manual.
  • Correct: type a new remaining amount in a pool's field. The pool keeps its place in the list.
  • Remove: the trash icon removes the pool and only its remaining points.

On the canvas, a token's Hit Points and Magic Points bars still show the ordinary points against the maximum. When the token has temporary points, a green dot is drawn at the right end of that bar, even when the bar is full. The token HUD shows the temporary total in green inside the Hit Points or Magic Points field, for example 7 +3. Typing into that field works like the sheet and chat: -3 is 3 points of damage or spending, taking temporary points first; +5 restores ordinary points up to the maximum; a plain number such as 4 sets the ordinary points, clamped to the maximum.

Amounts are whole numbers of 0 or more, with no upper limit; anything else is rejected. A pool at 0 stays listed until you remove it. On an unlinked token's sheet the pools belong to that token only; on a linked token they belong to the actor.

Earlier versions of the system could store a current value above the maximum, hidden on the sheet. When a world is upgraded, or an old actor is imported, any Hit Points or Magic Points stored above the maximum become one pool called Recovered excess for that resource, so no points are lost. It works like any other pool: correct or remove it once you know where those points came from.

Tabs

  • Skills: skills grouped by type, custom skill groups, and all special abilities. Group headers show the total of advances.
  • Combat: initiative, combat skills each followed by the weapons that use it, ammunition, resistances, armour and combat abilities. A weapon whose skill the character does not have is not listed here; it is still on the Equipment tab.
  • Equipment: money, total encumbrance, weapons, armour and other equipment.
  • Magic: magic skills, resistances, magic abilities, and spells grouped by type, with custom spell groups last. See the Magic page.
  • Effects: the character's effects. See Effects below.
  • Notes: free text.

Working with items

  • Click an item's icon to send it to chat. Hover over its name to see the description and traits.
  • Click a name or value to roll a test, or a damage formula to roll damage. See the Rolls page.
  • The + button in a group header creates a new item of that type and opens its sheet.
  • The edit icon opens the item sheet. The trash icon deletes the item immediately, without confirmation.
  • Skill advances can be edited directly in the list. Typing a negative number sets the skill total instead: -65 makes the skill 65%.
  • Drop items from the sidebar or a compendium onto the sheet. Dropping a whole item folder adds every item in it, including subfolders.

Equipment and money

Weapons, armour and equipment have a state, shown as an icon in the Equipment tab. Click the icon to change it: weapons can be Readied, Carried, Stored or Natural; armour Worn, Carried, Stored or Natural; equipment Readied, Carried or Stored.

Total Encumbrance counts the items that are Worn, Readied or Carried; ammunition and consumables count once per unit. It is shown against the limit of STR + SIZ. No penalties are applied automatically.

Money has one field per coin from the Coins Configuration setting. Consolidate Coins exchanges all money into as few coins as possible. Item costs are shown in coins, for example 1GD 5SP.

NPC

The NPC sheet is a compact version of the character sheet. The header holds the name and a short description; the edit icon next to it opens an editor for that description.

Characteristics

Characteristics are edited directly on the sheet. Under each value is a roll formula, by default that of an average human: 3d6, or 2d6+6 for SIZ and INT. The dice icon rolls all formulas and updates the values, without a chat message.

Attributes

Attributes work as for characters, temporary Hit Points and Magic Points included, except that NPCs have no Fortune or Growth Points.

Tabs

  • Details: skills and general abilities, magic (magic skills, abilities and spells, followed by the custom spell groups), equipment with quantities, initiative, a combat section with resistances, weapons, combat abilities and armour, and the NPC's effects (see Effects below).
  • Description: free text.

NPC sheets have no add buttons: drop items onto the sheet. There is no money or encumbrance tracking.

Effects

An effect records an ongoing benefit or restriction on a creature: a spell, a blessing, a curse or a ruling. It can change values on the sheet, and it can simply remind everyone of a rule. Effects never rewrite the actor's stored values: they are applied each time the sheet is calculated, so disabling or deleting an effect restores the values it changed.

Effects list

Characters have an Effects tab; NPCs have an Effects section at the end of the Details tab. Each row shows the effect's icon, name, source and state. Click the name to show or hide its rules text. The toggle enables or disables the effect, the edit icon opens the effect editor and the trash icon deletes the effect immediately, without confirmation. Disabled effects stay in the list, marked Disabled, and change nothing until enabled again.

The + button creates a new effect and opens it in the editor. Anyone who can edit the actor can manage its effects. On an unlinked token's sheet the effects belong to that token only; on a linked token they belong to the actor.

Effect editor

  • Name and icon in the header.
  • Source: where the effect came from, such as the spell's name. It is plain text, so it stays readable even when the spell is gone.
  • Disabled: the same as the toggle in the effects list.
  • Rules text: what the effect does in play, shown when the row is expanded in the effects list.
  • Changes: the values the effect changes. Add change adds a row; the trash icon removes one. Each row has a target, an operation and a value. The editor offers only the targets the system supports: Armour points and Movement rate. Both have these operations:
    • Add: adds the value; a negative value subtracts.
    • Multiply by: multiplies the value without effects. For example 0.5 halves it. Armour points round down; movement keeps the fraction, such as 7.5m.
    • Set to: replaces the result with the value.
    A movement rate row also has an optional Minimum: the rule's floor, such as Slow's 1m. Changing a row's target saves the effect, so the row shows the fields of its new target.

When several changes affect the same value, multipliers apply first to the value without effects, then additions, then a Set to replaces the result. If several Set to changes apply, the highest is used. Last, effects cannot take the value below the highest minimum of the changes that apply. A minimum never raises a value that is already lower without effects: Slow on a creature with Movement Rate 0 leaves it at 0.

An effect with no changes is a reminder: it only shows its rules text, for example Owes the river spirit a favour.

Effects created by macros or modules before this version may contain changes the editor does not manage. They are listed under Other stored changes not managed by OpenQuest and saved back exactly as they are; their behaviour is not guaranteed.

Values changed by effects

A value changed by effects shows a wand icon next to it. Hover over the value to see the value without effects and each contributing effect, with its source and amount, such as +3, ×0.5, = 8 or −6 (minimum 1). When a value combines multiplied and added amounts, or several Set to changes, the tooltip asks you to check which of them apply: the system does not decide whether two spells may stack.

Effect armour points are added on top of the Armour Worn calculation above: the best Worn or Natural armour plus the mod.

Recipe: Protection

Protection gives one extra armour point per point of magnitude. To record Protection 3 cast on a character wearing 2 AP leather:

  1. Open the character's Effects tab (on an NPC, the Effects section) and click +.
  2. Name the effect Protection 3, set the source to Protection and paste the spell's description into the rules text if you like.
  3. Click Add change and choose Armour points, Add, 3.
  4. Click Submit Changes. Armour Worn now shows 5 with the wand icon; its tooltip lists 2 without effects and Protection 3 (Protection) +3.

When the spell ends, disable or delete the effect and the armour points return to 2.

Recipe: Mobility

Mobility adds 2 metres of movement per point of magnitude. To record Mobility 2 on a character with Movement Rate 15, create an effect named Mobility 2 with source Mobility and add the change Movement rate, Add, 4. Movement Rate shows 19 with the wand icon.

Recipe: Slow

Slow takes away 2 metres per point of magnitude, but never reduces movement below 1 metre. To record Slow 3, create an effect named Slow 3 with source Slow and add the change Movement rate, Add, -6, with Minimum 1. A Movement Rate of 15 becomes 9; Slow 8 (-16, minimum 1) gives 1.

When Mobility and Slow are both on a creature, both apply and the tooltip lists both (15 + 4 − 6 = 13). Whether the spells really combine is the GM's call: disable or delete the one that does not apply.

Initiative

The Initiative field selects a skill or special ability with a formula, and the Mod field adds a whole number to it. The result is the actor's initiative; no dice are rolled. The same values are edited from the combat tracker; see the Combat page.

Roll data

Actors provide values for roll formulas, in skill formulas, damage formulas and chat commands:

  • @str, @con, @siz, @dex, @int, @pow, @cha: final characteristic values.
  • @dm: the damage modifier. It already includes its sign, so write 1d6 @dm, not 1d6 + @dm.
  • @skills.<slug>.value and @skills.<slug>.mod: a skill's total and mod, for example @skills.close-combat.value.
  • Other actor data by its path, for example @attributes.hp.value, @attributes.mp.max or @attributes.ap.value.
